Send the file that doesn't fit

Outlook says: “Try putting the file in a shared location and sending a link instead.” We say no. We build tools that cut a 24.7 MB quarterly report into six pieces, deliver every byte to the recipient, and never ask anyone to click a OneDrive link.

Why we exist

Your mail client never tells you the truth

“Attachment too large” is a remarkably vague accusation. Between 20 MB and 35 MB sit two product lines, three tenant configurations, one Exchange administrator you have never met, and a policy written in 2003.

📏

State the limit out loud

Outlook.com 20 MB, Exchange Online 35 MB by default, on-premises Exchange often 10 MB. data/limits.json records the real numbers, each with a method and a date.

✂️

Splitting is the honest fix

att split cuts a file into pieces the recipient never notices, verifies every shard with SHA-256, and ships a rebuild script that needs nothing installed.

🧾

Translate the bounce

att ndr reads a rejection and names the gate: your submission server, the recipient's gateway, or the one in the middle you did not know existed.

Where we stand Share links are fine — but recipients do not click them. Our goal is not to abolish OneDrive. It is to keep a path open for when the actual file has to arrive.

Four tools, one obsession

MIT licensed, no runtime dependencies, small enough to read over lunch.

splitter v0.1.0 · works today

Splits by any limit, writes a manifest with per-shard SHA-256, generates rebuild scripts for Windows and POSIX. In this repo: cli/att.mjs — one file, no dependencies.

ndr-translator stable
