One message, three limits

“What is the limit?” has one honest answer: it depends which link of the chain you are standing on. This article takes those links apart, including the reason the same error code keeps appearing right after you relax the configuration.

The short answer first

  • Outlook.com (consumer): the attachment limit is normally 20 MB.
  • Exchange Online (Microsoft 365): the organisational default is normally 35 MB, adjustable by an administrator, and inbound and outbound can be two different values.
  • On-premises Exchange: the factory default is commonly 10 MB; the real value depends on the mood of your predecessor and that year's compliance requirements.

None of those three numbers is wrong. They are simply answering different questions. And when a user presses Send, there is only one question in their head: will this message get through.

How many gates one message has to pass

Think of a message with an attachment as a journey. It passes at least five gates, and every gate has its own scale:

  1. The mail client: usually no limit at all. It just encodes the file you dragged in as part of the message body.
  2. Your submission server (Outlook.com or Exchange): the first real line of defence.
  3. Corporate outbound gateway / security scanning: often the lowest gate of the lot, and almost nobody knows its limit.
  4. The recipient's inbound gateway: another company, another rulebook, another phone nobody answers.
  5. The recipient's mailbox service: the last gate, and the one you hear about least.

Which error code you see depends on which gate stopped you — and the wording differs completely between products. That is where “one problem, ten descriptions” comes from.

Why relaxing the configuration does not help

Three common causes, ordered by how often we see them:

1. You changed outbound, and inbound is what stopped you

Raising the outbound limit from 25 MB to 50 MB only settles “can you send it”; whether the other side can accept it is decided by the other side's policy. The bounce is usually a recipient-side rejection such as 552 5.2.3.

2. Encoding makes the message bigger

Attachments are Base64-encoded in transit, which inflates them by about 33%. A 24 MB file looks like roughly 32 MB to the server. Most people count the file size; the server counts the encoded size. That is the whole gap. 

File 24.0 MB
After Base64 encoding ≈ 32.0 MB   ← this is the number you actually hit
Headers + body + signature ≈ another +0.2 MB

3. Policy takes time to apply, or the layer you changed is overridden by a higher one

Mail flow rules, connector policies, tenant-level settings, user-level settings — they all have precedence. Retrying immediately after the change makes it very easy to walk away convinced that “changing it made no difference”.

A self-check list you can actually follow

SymptomLikely culpritCheck first
550 5.3.4Your submission server / outbound rulesOrganisation-level message size limit and outbound connectors
552 5.2.3The recipient's sideTheir domain — not yours to change; you can only send less
0x80040610Rejected at submissionRedo the arithmetic with the encoded size
0x8004210BSend timed outAttachment size + upload bandwidth; usually queuing rather than a rejection
Stuck at 99%, no messageThe client is uploading / validatingWhen your patience runs out, switch to sharding

Measure it once, then change it

Our advice is a single sentence: before you change any configuration, measure the lowest gate on your chain. One binary-search run with splitter is enough — start at 5 MB and double the size, and record the first size that bounces. That is your real limit. 

$ att probe --to you@example.com --from 5MB --step double
5 MB    ✓ ok
10 MB   ✓ ok
20 MB   ✓ ok
40 MB   ✗ 550 5.3.4

Verdict: your effective limit is between 20 and 40 MB. Base64 inflates payloads, so split at 20 MB.

Write it down, put it in your internal documentation, and date it. Six months from now, when the product changes its default, that record will be the most useful thing you have — and it is also exactly how our limits database works.

Defaults quoted here vary with product and tenant configuration; trust the official documentation and your own measurements. Data on this page last verified 2025-11; method in the limits database.
