FAQ

The seven most-asked questions

Do you actually exist?

This site is a demo site for a fictional open-source society, built to show the structure and copy of a multi-page static website. The error codes and limit figures come from public sources, but numbers like The tool figures (6 executables, 48 checks, 7 services) are real and verifiable in the repositories; anything that looks like a vanity metric is not used here.

Why is the name so long?

Because the error message is that long. The name comes from Outlook's line “The file you're attaching is bigger than the server allows.” We pulled the adjectives out and used them as the society's name. Say it quickly and it becomes “att”, which is also the name of the CLI.

Are you connected to Microsoft?

Not at all. No sponsorship, no authorisation, no internal documentation. We are just users — the kind who read error codes properly.

Can you raise my limit?

No. Only your Exchange administrator or your mail provider can change a limit. What we can do is measure the real limit for you, split the file, and tell you exactly who is blocking you when it will not send.

Shards or a shared link — which should I use?

It depends on whether the recipient clicks links. A colleague with a shared drive who clicks links anyway: a link is less work. An outside client, phone only, or someone who has explicitly said “send me the attachment”: shards. Our default advice is shards first, because they do not depend on the other person doing anything.

What should I include in a repository issue?

The raw error (plain text preferred, a screenshot is fine), the actual file size, the encoded size if you can measure it, the provider and product version, the date it happened, and what you tried. For an issue that says nothing but “sending failed”, there is an 80% chance our only possible reply is “please add more information”.

Can I send you an attachment over 20 MB?

No. Really, no. Split it, or give us a way to download it. We have already seen enough of those emails in our own inboxes that postmaster has become the sender we know best.

Still stuck?

Send us the raw error, the rawer the better

Do not rewrite it, do not tidy it up, do not delete the lines that look useless — those lines usually carry the most information.
